What changed

A calmer, clearer path from “what do you do” to “get a quote.”

The structure and copy follow the approved direction. The work in this round is hierarchy, pacing and the ownership story, not a rewrite of the message.

  1. Split hero with the commitment card

    Headline and actions sit beside real charger photography, with “we don’t install and walk away” attached to the image instead of buried below it.

  2. Proof strip that earns its space

    Five numbers, one line each, counting up once as the strip enters view. No badges, no invented claims.

  3. Services as an alternating story

    L2 and L3 each get a photo, a short paragraph, four proof points and one action, so the two offers read as distinct products.

  4. Ownership models you can compare

    The three models are one interactive group: a “best for” line is visible on every row, and opening one closes the others.

  5. Fluid type and spacing

    Headlines scale with the viewport instead of stepping at breakpoints. Section spacing follows an 8-point scale, with more air around the hero.

  6. Interaction kept quiet

    Slow accordion easing, a gentle scroll-linked hero, and no bounce, spin or scroll reveals. Everything stops for reduced-motion visitors.

Please review

Four things worth a close look.

If something feels off, reference the section name. That is enough for us to locate it.

1Message and hierarchy

  • Does the hero answer “what is this and who is it for” in one read?
  • Is it clear that L2 and L3 are separate offers?
  • Should the proof strip sit this high on the page?

2Copy accuracy

  • Confirm the five headline figures are current and public.
  • Check the “best for” lines on the three ownership models.
  • Check the two new labels: “Most popular” and the L3 “Featured service” tag.

3Mobile

  • Switch the preview to Mobile and walk the full page.
  • Check the ownership accordions and the comparison table.
  • Confirm tap targets and the sticky header feel right.

4Detail and brand

  • Photography: are these the right sites and the right season?
  • Green, navy and yellow: enough restraint, or too quiet?
  • Anything that reads as a promise the business cannot keep?

Open decisions

Five things we need from you before launch.

These are recorded as open in the project notes. None of them block the design review; they do need an answer before the page goes live.

Who is the primary audience?

The page currently leads with property owners, fleet operators and site decision-makers, with drivers supported in the nav and footer. Confirm that order is right.

Needs an answer

Charging as a Service revenue split

The site copy states a 90% / 10% split. Confirm it is current and approved for public use, or we remove it.

Needs an answer

Sign-off on two new labels

“Best for” lines in the ownership accordions and the “Most popular” accent on Charging as a Service are new copy written for this layout.

Needs sign-off

Removed bullet lists

Two bullet lists were removed from the ownership accordions on request. The text no longer appears on the page, which is a known SEO trade-off. Keep or restore?

Risk to decide

Figures and territories

12 years, 10,000+ ports, 40 states, 4 provinces, 3,500+ chargers, 98% uptime and 500k+ sessions are taken from the current site. Confirm each before launch.
